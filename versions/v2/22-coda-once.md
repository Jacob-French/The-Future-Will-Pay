---
title: Once
label: Coda
---

> It isn't forever for the one inside. It's once.
> — Ione Tally

There is a moment.

In it, a man is kneeling in the dust at the far end of the highest room in the world, with his hand on a small brass wheel.

The wheel is warm. It has just turned, a single tooth, a little to the left, because the needle was drifting and he saw which way it would go next and got there first. Now the needle is on the line. Exactly on it. Not a hair either side. It is the best he has ever held it. It will never be held better.

Through the great window, the Lamp has begun. At its heart, where the hard white point was, there is the first edge of something — a thread of gold, the very first thread, the first instant of a light that would have been bright enough to end the world. It is only just beginning. It is always only just beginning. The Lace hangs round it, singing, every branch of it lit gold at the tips, as if a frost had caught the morning.

And down, and to the left, past the glare, on the black lattice of Spindle Four, below the Breach, there is a small soft light.

Hers.

It's going down. Steadily. A flight of stairs, in the Lees, out of the Fold, the way it should. Safe.

And just before this — it's here too; everything is — the light stopped on a walkway, and a small grey arm came up. The palm out. Held there, toward the Rim, toward the Crown, until it shook. She was looking up at him. He saw it. Of everything, it's the thing he sees most clearly, clearer than the needle, clearer than the gold.

He saw.

He isn't counting. That's the first thing he would notice, if there were a next thing to notice after the first. For the first time since 02:14 on the morning of Continuance Day, for the first time in fifteen years, perhaps in his life, there is nothing to count. Not the hammer. Not rungs. Not seconds. There is one of everything. There is one moment, and he is in it, and it is one.

*Count me*, she'd said. *I'm one.*

Everything is here.

That's the other thing. Nothing has to be remembered, because nothing is behind him; it is all simply here, in the moment, the way the whole Vault was in every thread of the Lace. Nell on the phone in a gym with a lizard in a box — *you don't know that; there might be*. Teo's tea, which was mostly a structural material. A boy on a flatbed hugging a cradle: *is there a form?* A palm on the other side of a pane of glass, and grease-pencil letters fading on it. Osk kneeling on the floor of a stone room: *you don't push up; you stack your bones*. An old woman with eyes like a bird, correcting him to the minute. Soup. Rat. A rooftop swaying with stamping feet and a cord tied round his wrist and taken off again. A hut in a Lee and a heart going too fast. *I'm glad it was you.* The cord tied round his wrist again, and this time left there — he can feel it; flat and neat and interlocking, like two hands clasped. *I'll hold your rope.* A visor pressed hard against his temple for a second, cold. *Haul.*

*So this is the start.*

*This is the start.*

All of it. At once. Not going. Not coming. Here.

And there is one more thing.

At the Keystone, beside him, very close — so close that she is kneeling almost where he is kneeling — there is a woman in a white coat. Not light. Not a ghost made of nine minutes going round. A woman. Thirty-eight years old, with her hair pinned up and a mole below her left ear and the side of her thumb raw where she's bitten it. She has just wrenched up a steel plate. She has just watched a key fall forward out of the world. It is 02:09 on the night of the Kindling, and she is looking up at the place beside the Keystone, at a man who isn't there, and he is there, and she is looking at him.

He turns his head and looks at her.

He knows her. He has read her whole life in pencil. He has watched her close her eyes for half a second a hundred and seventy-five thousand times, through someone else's eyes. He knows that she is about to pull a hammer and go down a lift and live for fifty years in a city of lamps and write letters to a little girl who will stay seven for all of them. He knows that she will marry a man who makes her laugh. He knows that she will think about this — about him, about this one look — every day for the rest of her life, and write it down once, in the thirty-first year, and never tell anyone.

He's sorry for her. He's glad of her. Both at once. He'd tell her, if there were time.

There isn't time. There's something better than time. There's this.

The needle is on the line. The gold is beginning. Her light is on the spindle, going down, and she raised her hand to him, and she is safe. He knows she is safe. He will always know it. That is the moment. That is the whole of it.

It isn't for ever. Not for him. It's once.

It is long enough.
