# Step 2 — The overall story (high-level plan)

**Title:** THE FUTURE WILL PAY
**Tagline on the cover:** *Remain at your station and await relief.*

## Premise in one breath
A city that has lived for ninety-one years on energy borrowed from the vacuum finds the loan
called in. A disgraced physicist goes into the broken power station expecting to die, crosses
a boundary where time runs seven hundred times faster, and finds a city of thirty thousand
people descended from the night shift — a civilisation that has been waiting 168 years for
relief. With a woman who has already lived through an eternity, he climbs toward the thing
that is about to end the world, to seal it inside a single moment. Someone has to stay in the
moment.

## Themes (what the story is *about* underneath the physics)
- **Debt and time.** The state borrows from the future ("The future will pay"). The Draw
  borrows from the vacuum. The cosmos itself may be a loan against nothing. Cas believes his
  guilt is a debt to be paid with his life. The story's answer: meaning is not repayment. A
  moment freely given owes nothing and is owed nothing.
- **Truth and calm.** Two regimes — the Directorate outside, the Stewardship inside — both
  choose calm over truth, and both are mirrors of Cas's own fifteen-year silence. His turning
  point is telling the truth to people who will suffer for hearing it.
- **Meaning vs dread.** Eternal recurrence as horror (Ione's Carousel: an open spiral where you
  remember every cycle) vs eternal recurrence as grace (a truly closed loop: lived once, for
  ever true). "It isn't forever for the one inside. It's once. It's only forever for everyone else."
- **Connection.** Everything important in the story is a loop between people that no one
  started: Ione's key, the figure on the spindle, the two of them pulling each other up.

## Shape
**Prologue — the Crown, 02:05–02:14.** Ruth Tally runs the Deepening Test under protest because
Deputy Minister Crane wants footage for Continuance Day. The Lesion tears. She tries the
Hollowell Clause; Crane won't give up the key; the gantry buckles; the key falls into the light.
For an instant she sees a man standing at the Keystone who isn't there.

**Part One — The Draw (outside, hours 6–33).** Cas is woken by the station's hammer and a Calm
Office car. The Ministry calls it an irregularity; Cas tells them it's a debt coming due and gives
them two or three days. He also tells them the part no one wants: if anyone survived near the
core, they've lived longer in there than the Commonweal has existed. The Ministry wants Tensioner
Six reseated so the Draw can continue. Cas knows that's impossible and says yes anyway because
yes gets him the suits. Three keel suits, three people: Cas; Teo Lisk (Civil Defence, secretly
under Calm Office orders to stop him from ending the Draw); Ben Farr (the kid who built the keels).
They prepare to die, badly and with jokes. Cas calls his daughter.
Inside, the station is a world: the Works' vertical maze, gravity weather, fractal filaments
threading the halls. Teo tells Cas about her orders and bins them. Ben is sealed into a blister of
racing time and lives eleven years in two hours of theirs — writing on the glass, mapping the
filaments, joking, grieving — before the blister accelerates and he dies in front of them. At the
edge of the Shear they find an ancient body in hand-sewn clothes that makes no sense. Cas's keel is
cracked. Teo gives him hers and stays in a Lee to wait. Cas crosses the Shear alone and climbs up
a conduit into light.

**Part Two — Holdfast (≈4 weeks inside; ≈1 hour outside).** A woman with a grey streak in her
hair is waiting at the top of the well and asks him what time it is outside. Holdfast: a city on a
keelstone plain under a burning Lamp and a spreading Lace, 31,000 short heavy people descended from
the night shift, a language of station jargon, a scripture that is an evacuation placard, rats. The
Stewardship proclaims him the Relief and asks him to tell the people everything will be all right.
He does — the old habit. Then the Tollers show him the Bell count (the Quickening: the end is
coming, faster) and Ruth Tally's true log (the test, Aldous Crane, the key). Ione tells him the Lamp
can be closed: there's a lock in the Crown, and she has its key. Nobody has ever believed her. Cas,
who has read Hollowell's Clause, knows she's right. Teo's messages arrive slowed across the ratio —
her minutes, his days — keeping the outside alive for him. Cas and Ione become friends in the
unhurried way of people who both expect to die. Then Cas does the thing he didn't do fifteen years
ago: he stands up in the Commons and tells Holdfast the truth. It costs lives (panic at the Drop
wells). Crane imprisons him and, in a long night conversation, makes the case for calm. Ione and the
Tollers break him out. They go up Spindle Four.

**Part Three — The Ascent (≈8 subjective days).** Every metre up, you weigh more; time runs faster; the
Bell stretches until the last one they ever hear. Ione's Lees. A gravity storm. Confessions: his
signature on the Margin Review; her three years. The Carousel — spatial loops and the trapped light of
the rupture's first nine minutes; they watch the Prologue happen in ghosts, and Cas finally understands
exactly what failed. Ione nearly doesn't get out again. The Lace — fractal, recursive, where things
half-exist; they must climb pressed together inside one keel bubble; they see a single figure coming
down in Cas's suit and each privately decides what it means; objects arrive before they're dropped;
Cas sees the bottom of the ledger (the universe as a loan against zero) and nearly comes apart; Ione
holds him together. The Crown. The key opens the Keystone and the plate reads THE WITNESS DOES NOT
RETURN. The Tear scores the key — the scratch Ione has run her thumb over for eleven years. She takes the
key back through a loop to the girl in the Carousel (who sees a grey-haired woman far off and thinks it's
her mother). They argue about who stays. Cas stays — not as penance, as choice. He gives her his suit;
the loop will begin when they arm the Keystone and end when her light is safe below. On the way down
Ione sees two small figures climbing and raises her hand. The seal.

**Part Four — Relief.** The Lamp goes out. Weight becomes ordinary. In the dark, the Bell comes at one a
second: Holdfast's time has rejoined the world's. Crane reads the placard one last time: "Relief has
come. We are relieved." Ione leads 31,000 people down through the Drop — no longer death. Outside it is
hour 37; the Draw is gone, so Averna is dark for the first time in ninety-one years, and both
populations see stars for the first time together. Teo, alive, meets Ione as though they're old
friends (she's heard about her for two hours). The Directorate tries to "process" the arrivals; there
are too many witnesses. Epilogue: weeks later, Ione and Teo bring Nell her father — months of recordings
he made for her in what was, for her, one evening — and the Tally Log goes public. In the dead Vault a
small black sphere with no weight hangs where the Lamp was: net zero, like the universe. Coda: Cas, in
the moment, for the first time.

## The ending (why this one)
- The reader is set up to expect "one of them comes back" and to assume it's Cas (he's seen the
  suit). It's Ione in his suit. The misdirection is fair: every clue is on the page.
- It isn't a suicide. His arc inverts the "suicide mission": he went in wanting to die to pay a
  debt, and he stays because he found a moment worth living for ever. The act looks the same;
  the meaning is reversed.
- The world is saved not by destroying the danger but by holding it one instant from happening,
  for ever — a sarcophagus of time. Fitting for a story about societies living on the edge of
  the catastrophes they've financed.
- Ione, who has lived an eternity, gets the one thing she never had: an ordinary future.
