# Step 5 — Revision of the whole plan

I read the plan end to end as if someone else had written it and asked: does it hold together,
is it honest about its own rules, is it interesting, does every piece earn its place? Changes
below have been applied to the world bible and chapter details.

## 1. The big one: the seal was logically broken — fixed
**Problem.** The plan had the sealed loop last "from arming until Ione's light passes the Rim",
with Ione inside at the start and gone at the end. A truly closed loop in time glues its last
instant to its first, so the contents must match. Ione present at the start and absent at the end
is a contradiction — and a living, remembering man can't be identical at both ends of a
ten-minute loop either. A reader who thinks about it would feel cheated. It also quietly mixed
two models of time travel (a "reset" model for the seal, a self-consistency model for the key).

**Fix — one model for everything: a single, self-consistent block of spacetime.** Nothing is ever
"reset". Loops are shapes, not replays.
- **The seal is an instant.** Hollowell's Clause folds the core into a closed loop of (near) zero
  duration: one instant whose future is itself. Nothing can differ between its start and end
  because there's no room for anything to happen. That's what makes it stable for ever, and it's
  why it needs a living hand up to the last moment: the witness holds the vernier to the incoming
  pulse by eye (no electronics survive there), right up to the instant, and is inside the instant
  when it closes.
- **What the witness experiences:** exactly one instant, containing everything he is at that
  moment — memory, love, the view. Not repeated; not endless; not remembered "again". *Once*. It's
  only "forever" from outside. This is the line the whole book is built to earn.
- **Cas chooses which instant.** Arming *calls the loan* (the unsayable word: Default) — the
  Settlement is forced to begin at the time set on the dial. He sets it for the moment Ione's light
  should pass the Rim far below: the instant he knows she's safe. That makes the climax a race
  (she has to get clear in time) and gives him the right last image.
- **Ione's Carousel is re-described to fit.** It was never a time loop for *her*. It's a spatially
  closed gallery (every exit leads back to the entrance) filled with *light* from the rupture's first
  nine minutes, trapped on closed paths and replaying. She lived forward — aging, remembering —
  while the ghosts repeated. So: the horror of repetition belongs to the *observer*, not the looped.
  "Ruth lived those nine minutes once. I'm the one who lived them a hundred and seventy-five thousand
  times." That sets up the ending exactly: once for Cas; forever for her to carry.
- **The genuine closed timelike curves** (the key, the figure on the spindle, the chalk arrows, Ruth's
  glimpse) all live in the Fold, which formed at the rupture, so nothing ever reaches back before
  hour zero. All are self-consistent. The key is a jinn made of keelstone (so it can't wear), and the
  one scratch it has always had is made in front of her.
- **Ruth's glimpse** (Prologue) of a man at the Keystone becomes a man in grey — the keel suit's
  liner, because he's given the suit away — with a knotted cord on his wrist (Ione's rigger knot,
  tied in ch.17). The Coda returns the glimpse from his side. It's the only moment where two ends of
  the story look at each other.

## 2. Bootstrap object in the Lace — swapped rope for chalk
A coil of rope looping through time would have to arrive un-frayed; fussy. Replaced with **chalk
arrows in Ione's own hand** marking the safe route through the Lace — marks she hasn't made yet.
Later, knowing the route only because she followed them, she draws them. Information with no author.
Cleaner, creepier, and a small rehearsal of the key.

## 3. The Tollers know more than the plan gave them credit for
Seven generations of people whose whole job is counting would have worked out that each Bell is one
second of the Outside. **The Tollers' secret faith: the Bell is the Outside's clock; they have been
counting the Outside's night.** Aldous Crane's doctrine says the Outside burned; the Tollers never
believed it, which is why they kept a letter to a seven-year-old for 168 years. When Cas says "hour
thirty-three", Perpetua corrects him to the minute. Cas isn't the revelation to them — he's the proof.
Better for Holdfast's dignity (they aren't primitives waiting to be told), and funnier.

## 4. Dates inside Holdfast straightened out
Ione: born 28 Holdfast-years ago; body ~31. Her mother **Seren Tally** was given to the Drop
**13** years ago (Ione 15) for publishing the true tally; Halloran Crane signed the order. Ione went up
at 17 (**11** years ago), came back two days later three years older, and has watched the wells since.

## 5. Teo shouldn't be "safe" just because we hear her voice
Her messages prove she's alive through Part Two, which would drain the fear from "lost". So the
**Interlude ends with the Lee shrinking**: the edge of the gradient crawling across the floor toward her,
the rotor-less suit, her backing into the last corner with her boots against the wall. Whether she
survives is held until Chapter 19, where her survival lands as a real surprise.

## 6. Pacing, Part One
Chapters 1–2 are both "before we go in". Kept both — Ben's death only works if we've loved him, and
the bureaucratic dread is part of the Chernobyl register — but each is trimmed to ~4,500 words, and
Chapter 2 is about *people* (the team), not equipment. The keel's spin-down note becomes a sensory clock
through the whole book: a hum that drops in pitch as the rotor runs down.

## 7. Making sure the ending is chosen, not a death wish in disguise
The risk: "man sacrifices himself" is the most predictable possible ending, and Cas *entered* wanting to
die. The plan now protects the inversion explicitly:
- Ch.12–13: he wants to live and says so (first to himself, then to Nell's recorder).
- Ch.15: the ledger — the worst dread in the book — and Ione's answer: zero isn't nothing, it's balanced.
- Ch.17: he's offered the thing he came in wanting, and has to refuse the old reason for it before he
  can accept it for a new one. "I came in to pay. I'm not paying anything. I want this." The misdirection
  of the figure (it's the suit, not the man) is what lets him see that the future was never about him
  being punished.
- He also quickly considers and dismisses alternatives (tie the wheel? the pulse wanders; it needs eyes
  and judgement), so it doesn't feel contrived.

## 8. Small things
- Holdfast doctrine: the key was "given to the Lamp so that it can never be put out" — so Ione having it
  is double heresy. Good.
- Ione tried to reach the Crown twice after her return; the Lace turned her back both times (it kills
  anyone without a keel). She's been waiting for exactly what Cas is wearing.
- The Carousel is the only route left to Spindle One's top — the Lace has closed the others. Explains
  why she must face it again.
- The truth in Ch.10 needs a *practical* payoff, not just a moral one: Cas tells them that if it works
  the Lamp will go out, and dark means the Drop has become a road. In Ch.18 that sentence is what keeps
  Holdfast from panicking in the dark.
- The hammer: after the seal it beats once a second; Civil Defence finally stops it on the fourth day
  (Epilogue) — the first silence in Holdfast's history.
- Ben's legacy pays off three times: sketchbook map through the Shear (5), the loop geometry in the
  Carousel (14), filaments in the Lace agree with it (15). And Jonah in the Epilogue.
- The last line of the book echoes Ben's: IT WAS LONG ENOUGH.

## What I'm keeping because it works
The debt spine (state, station, cosmos, man); the two mirrored regimes; the Bell's evolution from tide to
heartbeat; Ben's lifetime behind glass; Ione as the only person who has already lived through what the
ending asks for; the fair misdirection of the suit; dark Averna and the first shared stars; "They come back.
Every night."
