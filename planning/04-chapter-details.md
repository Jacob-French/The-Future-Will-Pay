# Step 4 — What happens in each chapter

(Beats, not prose. Lines in quotes are candidate lines, free to change in the writing.)

## Prologue · Nine Minutes — Ruth Tally
- 01:52, the Crown atop Spindle One. Night shift, the eve of Continuance Day. Ruth Tally, 38, shift
  engineer, fourteen years in the Crown. Her daughter Maddy (7) asleep outside with Ruth's mother.
  The Crown: a keelstone shell, a curved window onto the Lesion (a hard white-violet point, calm,
  "like a nail of light"), consoles, the Keystone housing in the floor that nobody ever looks at.
- Deputy Minister Aldous Crane with a camera crew: the Second Deepening will be announced at dawn,
  with footage. He wears the Minister's key on a chain for the shot (ceremonial).
- Ruth objects: Tensioner Six's bearing runs hot (maintenance deferred three quarters running);
  the test protocol requires the interlocks off. Crane: export contracts; the Minister; a mention of
  Maddy's school placement, lightly, which is how threats are made in the Commonweal.
- She does it. Procedure as liturgy: call-and-response, torque readings. Output climbs. The Lesion
  "sings" through the floor. The wall clock starts to run fast.
- 02:05:12 — Six unseats. The Lesion's edge breaks into lace. Time goes wrong in the room: a cup falls
  slowly, a man's shout arrives before his mouth opens, people in different parts of the Crown move at
  different speeds. Weight surges.
- Ruth goes for the Keystone: the Hollowell Clause — THE SEALING REQUIRES A WITNESS on the plate. It
  needs the key. Crane won't. "You'll end the Draw." "There won't be a Draw." He hesitates too long —
  the gantry buckles as a filament touches the Crown frame, the chain snaps, the key falls through the
  grating toward the light and is gone.
- For an instant she sees a man at the Keystone, dressed in close grey like a diver, a knotted cord on his
  wrist, one hand on the vernier wheel, calm, as though he has always been standing there — and he sees
  her. Then no one.
- 02:14 — she orders the Crown abandoned and, last out, pulls the Dead Man's Hammer (the station's
  unkillable last alarm, meaning *nobody is at the controls*). The lift down. The wall clock spinning.
  End on the first strike of the Bell.

# Part One · The Draw

## 1 · The Standby
- Open: Cas sitting fully dressed on his bed in the Standby flat (Tier 9, the Pit's rim) at 06:20,
  counting the hammer. He's been counting since 02:14. The phone that has never rung doesn't ring.
  A Calm Office car comes instead (no phone records).
- Colonel Gaunt in the car: soft voice, ginger biscuits, knows about the Margin Review. Menace as
  courtesy.
- Averna at dawn on Continuance Day: bunting, the Tending anthem on public speakers, Quota clocks going
  blank one by one, "margin irregularity" broadcasts, calm crowds with a thin skin, queues at Line
  stations. Over the Pit: a shimmer like heat over a road, but vertical, and birds that won't fly over
  it. Clocks at the cordon are twelve minutes fast.
- The Collar crisis room. Minister Ondine Rask; frightened engineers; data. A drone sent into the Works
  came back after 40 minutes with 11 hours on its clock. The last Crown telemetry: the Lesion boundary's
  dimension reads 2.7 — fractal. Cas goes cold.
- He explains: quantum interest as a loan; the Rollover; ninety-one years of compounding; the Settlement.
  "Two or three days." Joules and Chicxulubs. Burying the Pit is pointless ("You can't bury a debt").
  Evacuation pointless (it's the planet).
- The aside nobody wants: if anyone survived near the core, time there has run so fast that they've been
  alive longer than the Commonweal has existed. Rask: "That will not be repeated outside this room."
- Cas mentions Hollowell's Clause; Rask: that paper was withdrawn. "The sealing requires a witness." The key
  was with Deputy Minister Crane, "who is on site." Rask's order: reseat Tensioner Six, restore Continuance.
  Cas says Six can be reseated from the Crown (a lie; he says what gets him in).
- Three keel suits exist. Major Teodora Lisk introduced: she looks at him the way you look at a ladder
  before you climb it.
- End: alone at a window, watching the shimmer, counting. Memory: a pen, fifteen years ago. He thinks: at
  last. It's almost a relief.

## 2 · Keel
- The Tallow Works lab: three suits in cradles like saints in niches. Ben Farr — 26, talks like a leaking
  valve, knows every bolt. "I'm coming? I'm coming. Right. Good. Fine. Is there a form?"
- The keel explained through Ben's hands: a keelstone rotor, frame-dragging, a one-metre bubble; 14 hours
  to spin up (why they can't go at once). Suits weigh like cathedrals. Two people can share a bubble if
  they're "closer than is professional."
- Radio across time ratios (Ben's relay pucks): "If one of us ends up in fast time, the rest will sound
  like whales."
- Cas sees Gaunt speaking privately to Teo across the hangar. Files it.
- The waiting hours. Suiting drills. The team's dinner in the lab canteen: gallows humour, survival odds
  worked out on a napkin (Ben insists on doing it properly), Teo's floods story. "Why are you here, Hale?"
  "I'm the Standby." "That's what. I asked why."
- Letters. Ben films one for Jonah. Teo writes two, to her sons. Cas can't write.
- Cas calls Nell (Sabine first; they're being "relocated for comfort"). Nell collects stars she's never
  seen: glow stickers, charts, names. "If the lights go out, will we see them?" "Maybe." "Look for some
  in there." "There aren't any stars in there, Nell." "You don't know that." He says one true thing: that
  it's dangerous and he's scared. She tells him that's allowed.
- Continuance Day address broadcast with old footage. The Tending anthem.
- Night: Cas watches the keels spinning, humming a note he'll hear for the rest of his life.
- End: hour 30. Suits on. Walking to Gate Nine through the empty Collar. The Bell.

## 3 · The Collar
- Gate Nine, hour 31. The Collar abandoned mid-shift: coats on chairs, a birthday cake, the irregularity
  memo on every screen. The emergency placard on a wall: SECTION 9 — REMAIN AT YOUR STATION AND AWAIT
  RELIEF. Teo: "They'll put that on my headstone."
- The Works: scale and texture. Rectifier halls a kilometre long, still singing. Ninety-one years of
  additions: signage in four typefaces, a shrine to Iris Hollowell with dead flowers, graffiti: THE FUTURE
  WILL PAY — WITH WHAT? Smell of ozone and algae. Rats (plant).
- Gravity weather: a corridor where down rotates and they walk the wall; a squall; a stairwell where weight
  doubles for three steps. Their suit clocks begin to disagree with the outside.
- The dead: a group of evacuating night-shift workers caught by a gravity shear at a stairhead — crushed
  flat against a wall in a way that looks painted. Teo covers faces with jackets. R-rated but unlingering.
- First filament: a thread of white-violet light crossing a hall, branching into itself. Where it passes,
  steel has rusted to lace; a potted plant beside it has grown to the ceiling and died. Ben measures the
  time rate beside it — ridiculous numbers.
- Rest in a lee. Teo tells Cas about Gaunt's orders (make sure he restores Continuance; force authorised)
  and flicks the order chip into a coolant channel. Cas tells her the truth: Six can't be reseated; the
  only chance is Hollowell's Clause; the key is probably lost with Crane; he doesn't know if it can be done.
  "So we're walking into hell on a maybe." "Yes." "Good. I hate certainties. They always want something."
- End: the route runs through Bioscrubber Hall B-7. A filament runs along its far wall.

## 4 · Blister
- B-7: an algae hall (tanks glowing green) with a long observation gallery of thick glass. The bulkhead's
  jammed; Ben goes in to cycle it by hand. A shift in the filament — the hall's rate jumps. The bulkhead
  seals. Through the glass Ben is suddenly a blur.
- His radio arrives as a squeal; the suit re-times fragments: panic, then calm. Estimated ratio ~48,000
  (a minute of theirs, a month of his). Opening the door would drag them across the blister's boundary —
  a small Shear. Ben's first clear message: "DON'T OPEN IT."
- Writing on the glass (grease pencil from his kit): HOW LONG. They write back, mirror-lettered: 4 MIN.
  Blink: 2 MONTHS. Blink: I'M OK. Blink: TELL JONAH. And the time-lapse of a life: a beard, algae farming,
  a pallet bed, pacing, a rat he's tamed and named MINISTER. Anger (scrawls, a cracked fist on glass).
  Then peace.
- He studies the filament running through his hall — years of observation — and writes the Tear's
  structure across the glass: branching ratios, how filaments follow the Lattice's stress lines, where the
  gradient must be gentlest. Cas copies everything into his paper sketchbook, frantically, as it appears.
- Cas and Teo on the floor, watching. Teo on her sons. The unbearable decision: they could go; they stay.
  Ben: GO. I'M FINE. HAD A GOOD LIFE. MOSTLY ALGAE.
- The filament thickens. IT'S SPEEDING UP. THANK YOU FOR STAYING. IT WAS LONG ENOUGH. He is thirty-seven
  for a minute; then sixty; then very old — a palm on the glass; Cas's palm on the other side; then a
  shape; then dust. The tanks bloom and die, the rat's bones, the hall becomes an ancient ruin in thirty
  seconds. The filament drifts on.
- After. Teo vomits in her helmet vent. Cas can't stop drawing.

## 5 · The Shear
- Numb march. A gravity squall in a vertical shaft; Cas falls; his keel housing cracks; the rotor
  whines — hours of spin left, not days. He hides it. Teo hears it.
- The Lattice: a black keelstone wall miles long, humming. Far off, Tensioner Six, tilted like a pulled
  tooth. Confirmation for Cas: it can never be reseated.
- Conduit 14's access hatch and the body: desiccated, twisted by Shear, dressed in hand-sewn patched station
  fabric, a tiny carved keelstone lamp on a cord. Dead for decades in a place where only hours have passed.
  It came *down*, from inside. Cas works it out aloud: people survived at the core, for generations. Teo:
  "Hale, what's in there?" "People. Or there were."
- The Shear through a viewport: a wall of trembling air, 2× to 700× in forty metres. Ben's map shows a
  seam. Cas's keel won't survive the crossing. Teo: swap cores. The argument. "Guilt's a lousy reason,
  Hale. Die for something useful or don't bother." She'll wait in Lattice Station 14-C (a lee, station
  air, water, rations). "Send me news. I'll be bored." The radio: his weeks, her minutes. She'll hear him
  as birds.
- The swap: brief, dangerous, done. A handshake that becomes a hug in suits. "Go on, then."
- The crossing: horror written from inside the body — his hand ahead of him in time; sounds in the wrong
  order; his heartbeat echoing before it beats; thoughts arriving finished. The conduit rises through the
  Anvil; he climbs a ladder under crushing weight, following Ben's seam. Teo's voice in his ear slows and
  deepens into a groan and stops.
- Light above. A hand reaches down.

# Part Two · Holdfast

## 6 · What Time Is It Outside
- A woman and a boy haul him out of a Drop well. She checks his seals like someone who has read about
  suits. First question: "What time is it outside?" "Thirty-three hours. And a bit." She closes her eyes,
  works something out, says, "Then it's sooner than they think." Pim: "Is he the Relief?" Ione: "He's a
  man in a suit. Get his legs."
- He can't stand: 1.4 g. They put him in a rigger's cart.
- First sight of Holdfast from the well rim (the Drop District on the Anvil's edge): a city of scaffolding
  and cargo frames stacked around eight black spindles that rise four kilometres into light; the Lamp, a
  hard point too bright to look at; the Lace spreading beneath it like frost on the sky; shutters; smoke;
  thirty thousand voices. The Bell passes through the floor as a long swell — and he recognises it: the
  hammer he counted all night, stretched into a tide.
- Ione: short, dense, rope-scarred forearms, grey streak, eyes that don't hurry, a black key on a cord at
  her throat (he notices; doesn't understand). She tells him: "They'll call you the Relief. Don't let them
  tell you what that means." And: "When they're done with you, find the Tollers." Vanishes before the
  Wardens arrive.
- Warden Osk and the Wardens. Awe. Crowds. Pim runs ahead shouting. Transport through the city: stocky
  pale people, old station signage as street names, shrines to the Lamp, the rat pens, a mural of the
  Founders — Aldous Crane holding up a key, "giving it to the Lamp so that it can never be put out."
- The Steward's House at the foot of Spindle One. First Steward Halloran Crane. "Welcome to Holdfast,
  Relief. You're a hundred and sixty-eight years late."

## 7 · Remain at Your Station
- Crane in private: intelligent, courteous, tired; a man of rituals. What is the Outside? Burned? Cas: it's
  fine; it's been thirty-three hours. Crane's stillness. "Is there relief?" "There's me."
- The chapel: the laminated Section 9 placard framed in keelstone, a lamp before it. The evacuation arrows
  painted as holy symbols; MUSTER POINT B. Cas doesn't know whether to laugh or cry and does neither.
- Holdfast's official history: the Kindling; the Founders; the Watch; the Outside ended; remain and await.
- Crane knows about the Quickening. He asks what it means. Cas tells him: the end, weeks. Crane had guessed.
  Crane asks him to tell the people it will be all right — they'll look to the Relief; let them be calm at
  the end. Cas asks about the key; Crane is shocked the Relief knows the word. Doctrine: the Founder gave
  it to the Lamp.
- The first address in the Commons: twenty thousand people; the public Bell count (false); a hymn that is
  the Tending anthem worn smooth by seven generations. Cas meant to be vague. He sees the children. He
  lies, kindly: we haven't forgotten you; keep the Watch. They weep and sing. Being loved for a lie feels
  wonderful. That's the horror.
- Ione at the edge of the crowd, not singing, no judgement in her face — only patience, which is worse. She
  mouths: *Tollers*.
- Quarters; Osk as minder; the weight; first recordings — to Teo, to Nell.
- End: a pebble on the shutter. Pim: "She says the Tollers want you. She says bring your notebook."

## 8 · The Tollers
- Night crossing (Osk sees and says nothing). Toller's Tower, built round the Bell Column: pendulums, water
  clocks, a wall of 119,000 tally marks. Perpetua Tally: "You're the Relief? You look like you need a
  sit-down."
- The true tally: Bell intervals over 168 years, a clean decay curve. Cas plots it in his sketchbook — it's
  the debt coming due. The Founders' weighing-stone (the Standard) shows the Lightening too. The Tollers'
  estimate of the end: forty to ninety rounds, and the Lace is quickening faster than the Bells.
- Perpetua: "My daughter worked that out. They gave her to the Drop for it." Ione's mother, Seren, thirteen
  years ago; Halloran Crane signed it.
- The Tally Log: Ruth's binder. Cas reads the entries for the test night (echo the Prologue from her
  pencil). The key falling. And a letter: *Maddy —*. Cas does the arithmetic out loud without meaning to:
  Maddy is seven. The Tollers already knew — they have always known each Bell is one of the Outside's
  seconds; they have been counting the Outside's night for 168 years, and Perpetua corrects his "hour
  thirty-three" to the minute. What they never had was proof the Outside survived. He is the proof.
  Perpetua cries for the first time in years.
- Ione from the roof: "The Lamp can be closed. There's a lock in the Crown. I have its key." Black keelstone,
  stamped HOLLOWELL · KEYSTONE · 0001. The key that fell. She found it on a gantry in the Carousel that she'd
  walked across four thousand times, where it had never been, and then was. Cas: a jinn. Novikov. The floor
  tilts.
- Ione has watched the Crown for three years in ghost-light; everyone calls her mad. Cas: "She isn't. I've
  read the paper that lock was built from." Hollowell's Clause. The outer plate. She's read it a thousand
  times: THE SEALING REQUIRES A WITNESS. Neither knows what it means.
- Why she hasn't gone before: she tried twice after; the Lace turned her back. It kills anyone without a keel.
  She looks at his suit. "You're the first thing that's come through the floor in my lifetime that could
  get through the Lace."
- Plan: Spindle Four (dead, unguarded); Ione must re-scout the lower Lees (they move with the Lightening);
  Cas must be able to stand. Weeks.
- End: dawn shutters opening; the Lace a finger's width lower than yesterday.

## 9 · Greenbread
- Weeks compressed. Relief by day (blessings, appearances, a baby held up to him), Tollers by night.
- Holdfast life: markets (greenbread, cricket flour, fungus, rat); Pim's rat pens (rats named after Stewards);
  a rigger's wedding under the Lamp; a funeral to the Tanks; children playing a game called Drop; forehead
  greetings; the language. Cas gets stronger; eats rat; it's fine; it's actually good.
- Ione and Cas working gear: humour, questions. She wants rain, the sea, animals, the sky. He describes stars.
  She has one star. She keeps finishing his sentences a second early, apologises: "Habit. I've heard a lot of
  conversations more than once."
- First telling, partial: after her mother's Drop she went up to find the edge of the world; three years; came
  back two days later. She saw, once, a grey-haired woman very far off and thought it was her mother. And
  once a figure in a strange suit coming down alone. She's watched the wells for eleven years.
- Teo's first message: the long groan over hours, then playback: "I leave you alone for four minutes, Hale,
  and you find a city and become a prophet." Real, funny, frightened.
- Cas records for Nell. For the first time in years he describes a day honestly.
- Pressure: Crane wants another address. The Lace touches a spindle top — a rigger crew caught, aged to
  bones in a breath. The public tally lies about it. Cas decides.
- End: "I'm going to tell them." "They'll put you in the Quiet." "Probably." "Good. I know how to get people
  out of the Quiet."

## 10 · The Second Address
- The Commons, the festival of the 120,000th Bell. Crane expects reassurance.
- Cas tells them: the Outside is alive and only a day and a half older; the Kindling was an accident, a test
  ordered by Aldous Crane; the Lamp is a debt; the Quickening is the debt coming due; weeks. There is a chance:
  a lock in the Crown, and a key held by Ione Tally — the woman they call mad was right. And the instruction
  that will matter later: if it works, the Lamp will go out; when the dark comes, don't be afraid; it means
  the Drop has become a road. "Nobody is coming. I'm what came."
- Crane cuts it; Wardens; the crowd splits: heresy, grief, belief (riggers, Tollers). A rumour runs faster
  than truth: *the Drop is open now*. A rush at the wells. People go over. The Shear takes them at the lip —
  seen from a gantry: bodies coming apart in impossible slow-fast ways. Forty-seven dead.
- The Public Tally board smashed; the Tollers post the true count.
- Osk arrests Cas, not roughly. Ione tries to reach him; flees.
- End: the Quiet — a sealed coolant tank, black, silent but for the Bell.

## 11 · The Quiet
- Dark. The Bell. Forty-seven. The pen. Dread in full: it's all debt and it all cancels; his truth killed
  people; nothing he does is anything but more interest. The old wish to be finished.
- Crane comes with a lamp. The long night argument. Crane: truth is a luxury for people with futures; my
  ancestor's lie kept two thousand terrified people alive for a hundred and sixty-eight years; what has your
  truth done in a day? Forty-seven. Cas: the lie killed Ione's mother; it meant nobody ever tried; you kept
  them alive so they'd die calm. Crane admits he signed the Drop order. He shows Cas a page from Aldous's
  private journal: *keep them calm; it's the only mercy I have left.* "Can you close it?" "I don't know.
  Nobody's ever tried." Crane leaves the lamp.
- Breakout: Ione, Pim, and Osk (his brother went over at the wells; he blames the lie, not the man who told
  the truth). Pim through the vents of the Steward's House for the suit. Flight across a shuttered city.
- Perpetua's goodbye at the Tower: to Cas, dry and fond; to Ione, a look that holds eleven years. She keeps the
  Log ("Somebody should be here when you come back with the end of it").
- The suit again; Teo's second message waiting in its buffer: she's heard about the address. "You told them.
  Christ, Hale. Good." Averna's lights browning out; she's checked on Nell through Civil Defence — safe. She's
  fine. She's bored. Bring them out.
- End: base of Spindle Four, four kilometres of black lattice rising into the Lamp. "Don't look up. It only
  gets taller."

## Interlude · Seventy Minutes — Teo
- Minute 0: watching Cas go into the conduit — he moves in stutters and then is gone.
- Minute 4: the first chirp — a week of him in a breath. She laughs and cries at once. A city. A prophet.
- The minutes: rations, the suit's catheter indignities, singing, talking to Ben out loud, her sons (Mattias,
  Joss), her husband dead eleven years, the floods. The Lee's edge shimmering; she shifts when it shifts.
- The hard line to the Collar: Gaunt asks whether Hale is complying. She lies to the Calm Office for the first
  time in her life: "He's reseating Six as we speak."
- More chirps: the Tollers, Ruth's letter to a seven-year-old (Teo sits with that one a while), the truth in
  the Commons, the Quiet, the breakout. She gets to know Ione through Cas's voice and hears him change —
  lighter, stranger, alive.
- Minute ~68: his last compressed message: they're going up; electronics will die; if it works the Lamp goes
  out and the Shear goes; come and get them; and a bundle marked NELL.
- Minute 70: she records her last message to him. We hear it from her side (he'll hear it on the climb).
- Then the Lee begins to shrink: the edge of the gradient crawling across the floor. No keel. She backs into the
  last corner, boots against the wall, and watches it come. "Come on, Hale." (Her fate held until Ch.19.)

# Part Three · The Ascent

## 12 · The Lees
- The climb: Spindle Four's lattice and dead ladders, Ione above on rope, Cas in the keel suit. Each hundred
  metres heavier. Ione reads Lees with chalk dust and a plumb bob; she knows their seasons.
- Teo's last message arrives: goodbye with jokes in it; her sons' names; "guilt's a lousy reason"; "bring
  them out." The last voice from outside.
- Looking down: Holdfast's shutters opening and closing faster and faster — rounds passing in minutes. They
  are living faster than the city.
- The last Bell passes up through the spindle. Ione counts it. "That's the last one we'll hear."
- Camp in a Lee. Ione's full story of the Carousel: seventeen; the gallery; nine minutes of ghost-light; every
  exit leads back to the start; 175,000 cycles; she learned the console, every word; the months on the floor;
  she decided to love the ghosts; Ruth became her closest friend, who never once heard her ("she bit her thumb
  when Crane talked"). The key: one cycle, something new. The grey-haired woman far off on the Rim. The seam.
- What the Carousel really was: not a time loop for *her* — a gallery closed on itself in space, full of the
  rupture's light trapped and replaying. She lived forward (aging, remembering) while the ghosts repeated.
  "Ruth lived those nine minutes once. I'm the one who lived them a hundred and seventy-five thousand times."
- Her rule: "If a loop is really
  closed, the one inside doesn't remember. It's once. It's only forever for everyone else." And: "In there
  nothing happened once. Out here everything does."
- End: he watches her sleep and wants, carefully, to live.

## 13 · Lamp-Weather
- A gravity storm on an exposed stretch: chalk falling sideways, then the surge — four g, zero g, sideways.
  Ione's rope parts; Cas drags her into his keel bubble; they ride it out pressed together.
- Shelter in a rigger's cache in a Lee. Suit electronics failing: clocks disagree, radio dead; the recorder
  still works.
- Cas's confession: the Margin Review, the pen, fifteen years of silence, Nell. "I came in here to die. It
  seemed tidy." Ione doesn't absolve him: "You think it's a debt. You keep paying and it keeps growing, because
  that's how your kind of debt works. You built a whole city on that idea." "I watched a woman make the worst
  choice of her life a hundred and seventy-five thousand times. She never got to make another one. You can."
- They make love — two people who expect to die, clumsy with suit seals and weight, tender and funny.
- End: Cas records for Nell: he wants to live. First time he's said it.

## 14 · The Carousel
- The gallery ring below the Rim. Ione calm and shaking.
- Ghost-light: the Crown's nine minutes replaying as light across the gallery. Cas watches the Prologue
  happen — Ruth, Crane, the crew. Crane refusing; the key. Ione mouths Ruth's words with her. Cas sees exactly
  what failed and why, and how the Keystone must be run.
- The spatial loop: they walk out and walk back in at the start. Ione's method needs something different;
  nothing's different. Cycles: 10, 50, 200. Ione starts slipping into the old months — sitting down, letting
  it happen through her. Cas holds her and keeps talking.
- Getting out together: Ben's branching ratio from the glass gives Cas the loop's geometry — where the seam
  must be; Ione's eye finds the shimmer where he points. Theory and practice. Ben gets them out.
- On the Rim, Ione breaks — first time he's seen her unguarded. "I thought I'd never have to watch her again."
- End: the Lace ahead like a forest of frost.

## 15 · The Lace
- The crossing to Spindle One's top through the Lace. One bubble, two bodies: "Hold on to me. Whatever you see."
- Recursion: gantries within gantries; a filament holds the whole Vault in miniature, including two tiny people
  looking into a filament.
- Causality slips: they hear their own voices before they speak; chalk arrows in Ione's own hand mark the
  safe path through the filaments — marks she hasn't made. Later, knowing the path only because she followed
  them, she draws them. "We've been here." "No. We're about to be."
- The figure: across the gulf, one person in a keel suit coming down alone. Cas's suit — Teo's patched housing,
  Ben's painted "3". It pauses and raises a hand. Cas thinks: it's me; I come back alone; she dies. He says
  nothing. Ione has seen it before. She says nothing. Both decide privately.
- Unexistence: Cas's hand thins; the gantry half-exists; for one second he can't remember who's in his arms.
  Terror. She's back. He holds harder.
- The ledger: looking into the Tear he perceives the bottom of things — every positive balanced by a negative,
  the universe a loan against nothing, all of it summing to zero. Nothing matters; it all cancels. He comes apart.
- Ione brings him back — his name, over and over; the ghost-light: "Everything in the Carousel cancelled. I loved
  them anyway." "Zero isn't nothing. Zero's balanced."
- The Crown's outer hatch. End: Cas resolves to be the one who stays, so the figure will be wrong.

## 16 · The Crown
- Inside: dust like snow, cups fossilised, the wall clock stopped at a time that means nothing. Ghost-light of
  Ruth at the Keystone — replayed light; she can't see him. (The real mutual glimpse is saved for the Coda.)
- The Keystone. Outer plate. Ione's key turns. The housing opens: vernier wheel, the witness gauge, arming lever,
  a dial. The inner plate: Hollowell's procedure, and the last line: THE WITNESS DOES NOT RETURN.
- Cas reads it and understands the machine: arming *calls the loan* — forces the Settlement to begin at the
  dialled time instead of whenever the Lace decides. At that instant the Fold closes around the core — debt and
  repayment together — into a loop of zero length: one instant whose future is itself. Up to that instant a
  witness must hold the vernier to the incoming pulse by eye and hand (no electronics live here; the pulse
  wanders; it needs judgement). The witness is inside the instant when it closes. Not a prison of repetition —
  one instant, lived once, true for ever. Default: the unsayable word. He feels calm.
- The scratch: as Ione draws out the key, a filament passing through the Crown floor touches it. A bright line in
  keelstone. The one she's run her thumb along every night for eleven years. It has always had it.
- The key must go back — nobody else can take it. The door: the Crown's lower gallery, where the ghost-light
  of the Carousel is not ghost but *then*. Far below, a girl lying on a gallery floor.
- Ione goes. Cas waits (seconds). She comes back after much longer for her: she put the key on the gantry; the
  girl looked up, saw a grey-haired woman far off, and called *Mam?* and Ione couldn't answer. She breaks.
- End: the inner plate between them. THE WITNESS DOES NOT RETURN. They both look at the suit.

## 17 · The Witness (Cas → Ione)
- The argument. Ione: "I've already lived my forever. You haven't lived your ordinary." Cas tells her about the
  figure. She saw it too, at seventeen; she's always known one comes down. They both assumed it was him.
- Then the realisation, together: they saw the *suit*. Ione: "No." Cas: "It was you. It was always you." "Then
  it's not a choice." "It's already true, and I'm still choosing it."
- The reasons: Holdfast needs someone to lead them down and they'll follow her now; Teo; Nell's voice; Maddy's
  letter; the Log. And the real one: not penance. "I came in to pay. I'm not paying anything. I want this."
- Choosing the instant: he'll set the dial for the moment her light should pass the Rim far below — the moment
  he knows she's safe. That's the instant he'll be in. "It's once. You told me. It's only forever for you."
- Gifts: she gives him her rigger's knot tied round his wrist; he gives her the sketchbook, the recorder, the
  suit. She's furious, then quiet.
- The last ordinary minutes before arming: what she'll eat outside, the sea, rain, a star she'll learn for him;
  a small joke; a last kiss. She suits up; it's too big; he adjusts the straps with shaking hands. He's in the
  grey liner. She ties the knot on his wrist.
- Arming. Hands together on the lever. "So this is the start." "This is the start." Now the clock is real: she
  has to be past the Rim before the dial runs out. She goes. He takes the vernier.
- POV → Ione. Down through the Lace alone. Across the gulf: two small figures climbing, pressed together in one
  bubble. She stops. She raises her hand.
- Through the Carousel — the seam where she knows it is. A race: the Lace stirring as the pulse gathers, weight
  surging. Past the Rim with seconds to spare.
- Above her the Lamp goes out.

# Part Four · Relief

## 18 · The Bell — Ione
- Darkness total but for the suit lamp. Weight ordinary — she feels she might float. Silence where the Lamp's
  hum has always been. Nothing visible above: black on black.
- The descent (normal time now; a long day of stairs). Holdfast below lit by oil lamps, points of fire.
- The city: fear, but they remember his words — *when the dark comes it means the Drop has become a road*.
  Osk and the riggers organising; Pim everywhere.
- Toller's Tower: Perpetua dying in her chair. The Bell comes at once a second now, like a heartbeat. Our
  time is their time. She laughs about it. She gives Ione the Log and the letter to Maddy. She dies holding
  Ione's hand between two Bells.
- The Commons: Crane before the placard. Reads Section 9. "Relief has come. We are relieved." He confesses
  Ione's mother publicly. Ione and Crane: not forgiveness; something drier. He'll walk out last.
- The walk down the Drop: ropes, lamps, children, rats in cages, songs. The Works in the dark. Ione passes B-7:
  the glass with Ben's faded writing. She leaves the carved lamp token on the sill.
- End: at the foot of Conduit 14 a woman with a torch: "You must be Ione. He never shut up about you."

## 19 · Stars — Teo → Ione
- Teo: the Shear's shimmer vanishing mid-breath; weight changing; she knows. Civil Defence arriving. Waiting
  at the conduit. A light coming down, a figure in Cas's suit — her heart — and it isn't him.
- Their meeting, from Teo's side: two women who know each other through a man who isn't there.
- Teo on the line to the Collar: "Send everyone. Bring water. Bring blankets. Bring a lot of blankets." Gaunt:
  "How many?" "Thirty-one thousand. Give or take a rat."
- The walk-out through the Works and the Collar: Civil Defence ropes and lights. Night. Averna is dark — the Draw
  is gone, the grid failed, the Canopy down. People on every tier of the Stacks come to the edges.
- POV → Ione at Gate Nine: wind (never felt wind); cold; the weight; the children jumping, amazed how high; and
  the sky — stars, thousands, the first ones she's ever seen and the first Averna has seen in ninety-one years.
- The Calm arrives to "process". Rask. Ione tells her what the Deputy Minister did, and how she knows. "Who are
  you?" "The night shift." Crane, last out, meets Rask: "It's over, Minister. We're relieved." Too many witnesses
  — six hundred million people looking up at the same sky.
- Dawn. The stars fade. Ione, alarmed: "They're going out." Teo: "They come back. Every night." "Every night?"
  — the good kind of repetition.

## Epilogue · Net Zero — Ione, weeks later
- Averna without the Draw: rationing, fusion stations restarting, arguments, a public inquiry, the Tally Log and
  Cas's Margin Review testimony (in his recordings) published; a poster with THE FUTURE WILL PAY and someone's
  spray-paint under it. Nothing is fixed. Things are *said*.
- The Relief Camp: quarantine, first colds, weather. Jonah (Ben's fiancé, a nurse) — Ione tells him about the
  glass. MOSTLY ALGAE. He laughs and then can't stop crying.
- Madeleine Tally, seven: a letter from her mother, written a hundred and sixty-eight years ago, delivered by
  her mother's great-great-great-great-granddaughter. Restraint.
- Nell and Sabine on a dark balcony. The recordings. Nell asks if he's dead. "No. He's in one moment. It's only
  once. It's just always true." "Is it a good moment?" "It's the best one I know." Nell teaches Ione a
  constellation. Ione tells Nell the star she's learned for him.
- The hammer finally stopped by Civil Defence on the fourth day — the first silence in Holdfast's history.
- The dead Vault: Ione and Teo with lamps. Where the Lamp was, a black sphere hanging in the air, weightless;
  the instruments say zero — net zero, balanced, like the universe. Ione climbs to it and lays her palm flat on
  it (Ben's palm on the glass). What she says.

## Coda · Once — Cas
- The instant, written from inside: nothing moves and everything is present. Her light, small, past the Rim. The
  vernier under his hand, the pulse held. The knot on his wrist. Everything he remembers is there at once — Nell's
  glow-stars, Ben's glass, Teo's laugh, Ione saying *this is the start*. A woman at the console in an old uniform
  looking straight at him (Ruth, at 02:05, across the Fold). Last line echoes Ben: it was long enough.
